Flashmath

Somewhere between the guide number printed on the box and the f-stop on your lens is a chart nobody carries. Flashmath replaces it: the inverse-square law is exact physics, the ISO and power rules are published, and the answer to "what aperture at 4 meters" should not require a spreadsheet or a prayer.

Run my numbers
  • Real reach - your flash's guide number is honest only at ISO 100, full power. See what it actually delivers at your ISO and your power setting.
  • Aperture from distance - the exact f-number for your subject distance, the nearest full stop, and how many stops of error that rounding hides.
  • Distance from aperture - how far the light really reaches at f/2.8, before you promise the group shot at the back of the hall.
  • Power needed - the exact power fraction for your aperture and distance, mapped to the 1/1...1/128 steps, with the honest shortfall when full power is not enough.
  • Bounce honesty - ceiling bounce costs stops, labeled as the rule of thumb it is instead of a fake precision figure.
  • The falloff curve - the inverse-square law drawn for your exact setup, so you can see why the front row is two stops hot.
Runs entirely in your browser. Guide numbers obey the inverse-square law (exact); the published conventions are GN at ISO 100 and full power, GN scaling with the square root of ISO ratio and power fraction. Bounce losses are labeled rules of thumb - your ceiling is not my ceiling.
